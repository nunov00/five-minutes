title: The Study Method That Feels Worse but Works Better
description: Rereading creates familiarity, while trying to recall an answer trains the skill you will actually need later.
takeaway: If you will need to remember without notes, practise without notes.
question: Why can rereading make you feel more prepared than you really are?
answer: Seeing the material makes it feel familiar, but recognising an answer is easier than producing it from memory.
searchTerms: [active recall, study tips, testing effect]
---
You read a page again and everything looks obvious. Then someone asks you to explain it without the page, and the answer disappears.

That gap exists because recognition and recall are different jobs. Rereading practises recognising words that are already in front of you. **Retrieval practice** means hiding the material and trying to produce the idea yourself.

In one study, students repeatedly reread passages or repeatedly tried to recall them. Rereading looked better on a test after five minutes. After two days and one week, the students who had practised retrieval remembered more.

The struggle is useful, but guessing forever is not. Try to answer, check the source, correct the gap and return later. Flashcards can do this, but so can explaining a lesson aloud, drawing its process from memory or answering its Test Yourself question before opening the answer.

**Takeaway:** the uncomfortable moment when you search your memory is part of the learning, not proof that learning failed.
