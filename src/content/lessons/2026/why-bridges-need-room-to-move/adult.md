The bump you sometimes feel at the end of a bridge is often part of a deliberate boundary. A bridge is not a single frozen object: its deck changes length as temperature changes, and it bends and rotates slightly as vehicles cross.

Materials expand when they warm and contract when they cool. As a rough rule, each metre of structural steel grows by about one millimetre when its temperature rises by 10°C. Across a long span and a wide seasonal temperature range, those small changes add up to substantial movement.

If the deck were locked tightly between rigid supports, the movement would not disappear. Restraint would turn it into internal forces that push on connections, piers and abutments.

## Put movement where it can be managed

An **expansion joint** creates a controlled break in the road surface. Its gap can narrow or widen while still carrying tyres across the deck. Seals help keep water and debris away from the structure below.

Under the deck, **bearings** transfer the bridge's weight to its supports. Some are fixed in one direction; others can slide, deform or rotate. Together, joints and bearings guide movement along intended paths instead of letting stress build wherever the structure is weakest.

Temperature is not the only cause. Concrete can shrink and creep over time, traffic bends the deck, and supports may settle. Engineers estimate the combined movement range before choosing the joint and bearing system.

## Why some bridges have no visible joint

Joints are useful but demanding. Leaking or damaged joints can let salty water reach bearings and beam ends, accelerating corrosion. Some shorter bridges therefore use integral or semi-integral abutments: the deck remains continuous, while the bridge ends and surrounding soil accommodate thermal movement.

That is not a bridge without expansion. It is a bridge that manages expansion somewhere else.

The deeper engineering idea is compliance: a structure can be safer when selected parts are allowed to move. Strength matters, but so does deciding where motion will happen.
