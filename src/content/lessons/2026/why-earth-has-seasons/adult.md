---
title: Why Summer Is Not Earth Moving Closer to the Sun
description: Earth's tilted axis changes both the angle and duration of sunlight in each hemisphere throughout the year.
takeaway: Seasons come mainly from axial tilt, which changes how concentrated sunlight is and how long each day lasts.
question: What two effects of Earth's tilt make a hemisphere warmer in summer?
answer: Sunlight arrives more directly and the Sun remains above the horizon longer each day.
searchTerms: [what causes seasons, earth tilt, summer and winter]
---
If distance caused the seasons, both hemispheres would warm and cool together. Instead, January brings winter to the Northern Hemisphere and summer to the Southern Hemisphere.

Earth's axis is tilted relative to its orbit. As Earth travels around the Sun, that axis continues pointing in nearly the same direction. For part of the orbit, the north tilts towards the Sun; six months later, the south does.

The tilted hemisphere receives sunlight at a more direct angle, concentrating the same incoming energy over a smaller surface. It also has longer days, giving the surface more time to warm. The other hemisphere receives more slanted sunlight and shorter days.

Earth's orbit is slightly non-circular, but the distance change is not the main driver. In fact, Earth is closest to the Sun in early January, during northern winter.
