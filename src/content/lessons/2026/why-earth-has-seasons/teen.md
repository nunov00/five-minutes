“Earth is closer to the Sun in summer” sounds reasonable until you ask: whose summer?

When Europe has July heat, Australia has winter. Both places travel around the Sun on the same planet, at the same distance. The difference is Earth's tilted axis.

As Earth completes its orbit, the axis keeps pointing in nearly the same direction. When the north tilts towards the Sun, northern sunlight arrives more directly and spreads its energy over less ground. Northern days also last longer. At the same time, sunlight reaches the south at a lower angle and its days are shorter.

Six months later, the pattern reverses. Earth's distance does change slightly during the year, but it is actually closest to the Sun in early January.
