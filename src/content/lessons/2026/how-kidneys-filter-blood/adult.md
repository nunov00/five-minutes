A kitchen sieve keeps large pieces on one side and lets small ones pass. A kidney begins in a similar way, but the important work comes next: it takes much of the filtered material back.

Each kidney contains microscopic working units called **nephrons**. Blood enters a knot of tiny vessels in a nephron called the glomerulus. Pressure pushes water and small dissolved substances through a filtration barrier, while blood cells and most large proteins remain in the bloodstream.

## Filter, reclaim, adjust

The filtered fluid then travels through a tubule. Cells along that tubule return needed water, glucose and salts to the blood. They also move some additional wastes and excess ions into the fluid. What remains becomes urine.

This filter-and-reclaim design lets the kidneys continually adjust the blood rather than merely remove a fixed list of wastes. They help control water, salts and acidity. They also release hormones involved in blood pressure, red-blood-cell production and bone health.

The useful mental model is therefore not a passive strainer. A kidney is a selective recycling and balancing system: it creates a broad first draft of fluid, then edits that draft according to the body's needs.
