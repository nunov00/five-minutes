Drop an ice cube into water and it floats. Yet it is still water. Why should becoming solid send it towards the surface?

The key is **density**: how much mass fits into a given volume. An ice cube contains less mass than an equal volume of liquid water. That lower density lets it float.

## The molecules need more room

In liquid water, molecules move past one another. Attractions between them keep forming and breaking as their neighbours change.

When water freezes at ordinary pressure, the molecules settle into a repeating pattern. Think of a crowd arranging itself into rings: becoming organised can leave more space, rather than squeeze everyone together. The comparison is about spacing; molecules are not people choosing where to stand.

In ordinary ice, the attractions hold the molecules in an open crystal structure. They still vibrate, but no longer move past one another as they do in the liquid.

No extra water has appeared, and the molecules have not inflated. The same amount simply occupies more space. That is why a frozen sample expands and why ice floats: both follow from how its molecules fit together.
