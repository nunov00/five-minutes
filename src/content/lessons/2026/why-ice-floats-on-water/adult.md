An ice cube floats in the substance it was made from. That familiar sight hides an unusual result: for most substances, the solid is denser than the liquid.

**Density** means mass per unit volume. Compare equal volumes of ordinary ice and liquid water: the ice contains less mass. Compare equal masses instead, and the ice takes up more space.

## Order can leave gaps

Water molecules attract one another. In liquid water they move past each other, continually changing neighbours. When water freezes at ordinary atmospheric pressure, those attractions organise the molecules into a repeating crystal structure with more space between them.

The molecules themselves have not grown. Their arrangement has changed. Nor have they stopped moving entirely: they still vibrate around their positions in the crystal.

The result is a solid whose structure is more open than that of its liquid. With the same mass spread through a larger volume, its density falls below the surrounding water's, allowing it to float.

This is also why freezing water expands. Ice floating in a glass and water occupying more space after freezing are two consequences of the same molecular arrangement. Becoming solid does not always mean becoming more tightly packed.
