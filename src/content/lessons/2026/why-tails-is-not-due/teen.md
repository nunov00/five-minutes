Five heads in a row can make a coin look as though it needs to put things right. But a fair, independently tossed coin has no memory of the score. Tails still has a one-in-two chance next.

Expecting the next result to fix the streak is called the **gambler's fallacy**. It confuses a pattern across many tries with a promise about one try.

## What “evening out” can mean

Imagine the next 100 tosses produce 50 heads and 50 tails. Add the first five heads: you now have 55 heads and 50 tails. Heads make up about 52.4% of the total, much closer to half than before, but the five-head gap remains.

That exact split is just an example. It shows how a percentage can move towards half without extra tails paying back the old streak.

Now put three red counters and three blue counters in a bag. Remove one red counter and keep it out. There are now two red and three blue counters, so blue has a three-in-five chance on the next random draw. Here the earlier result changed the bag.

That is the question to ask: did the process change? Real matches, weather and card draws are not automatically independent just because coin tosses can be. Check the connection rather than assuming every streak must reverse.
