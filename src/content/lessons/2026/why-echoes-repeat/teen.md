---
title: Why a Wall Can Repeat What You Say
description: A clear echo is your own sound returning after a reflection and a short journey.
takeaway: You hear an echo when reflected sound returns late enough to separate from the original.
question: What creates the pause between a shout and its echo?
answer: The sound must travel to a reflecting surface and then travel back to the listener.
searchTerms: [how echoes work, reflected sound, echo delay]
---
Shout near a distant wall and the wall may seem to answer. It is not making a new sound; it is returning part of yours.

Sound travels outward through the air. When it reaches a hard surface, some energy reflects and travels back.

A nearby reflection returns so quickly that it blends into the original sound. A more distant surface gives the sound a longer round trip, creating a pause that your ears can notice.

Soft materials absorb more sound, while large hard surfaces tend to return more of it. The clearest echoes therefore need both enough distance and a useful reflecting surface.
