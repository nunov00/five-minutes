---
title: Why an Echo Repeats Your Sound
description: An echo arrives when reflected sound takes long enough to return as a separate event.
takeaway: An echo is delayed reflected sound, with distance creating the delay.
question: Why do distant walls produce clearer echoes than nearby surfaces?
answer: Sound takes longer to travel to a distant wall and back, making the reflection easier to distinguish from the original sound.
searchTerms: [echo, reflected sound, sound delay]
---
A shout sends pressure changes through the air. Some of that sound reaches a surface and reflects back.

If the returning sound arrives quickly, it blends with the original and changes the room's character. If it arrives after a noticeable delay, the listener hears a separate echo.

Distance creates the delay because sound needs time to travel to the surface and back. Hard, broad surfaces usually return more sound than soft or irregular ones.

That is why a cliff or an empty hall can repeat a sound clearly while curtains and furniture make a room less echoing.
