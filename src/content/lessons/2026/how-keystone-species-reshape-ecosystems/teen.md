---
title: How Sea Otters Protect Kelp Without Eating It
description: By eating sea urchins, otters can change grazing pressure on kelp and trigger effects throughout a food web.
takeaway: A species can shape an ecosystem through indirect effects on organisms it never eats or touches.
question: What chain connects sea otters to healthier kelp forests?
answer: Otters eat sea urchins, fewer urchins graze on kelp, and more kelp can survive to support the wider ecosystem.
searchTerms: [sea otters and kelp, trophic cascade, keystone species explained]
---
Sea otters can protect a forest even though they never plant it, water it or eat its leaves.

The forest is kelp growing underwater. Sea urchins eat that kelp, while otters eat sea urchins. When otters become scarce, more urchins can survive and graze heavily. The kelp forest may shrink, affecting fish and other animals that use it for food or shelter.

This chain reaction is a **trophic cascade**. Otters can act as a **keystone species**: their effect on the ecosystem is much larger than their numbers might suggest.

Real food webs contain many overlapping relationships, so otters are not a magic switch. Local conditions, disease, climate and other predators also matter. The example is powerful because it shows why counting only direct effects misses much of ecology.
