Start with one unit and double it ten times. After five doublings you have 32—only about three per cent of the final 1,024. Half the time has passed, but almost all the growth is still ahead.

Linear growth adds a fixed amount: 10, 20, 30, 40. Exponential growth applies a fixed proportion to the current amount: 10, 20, 40, 80. The increase itself therefore grows.

## Think in doubling times

For a process with a constant proportional growth rate, the time required to double stays constant. The jump from 100 to 200 takes as long as the jump from 100,000 to 200,000, even though the second adds vastly more.

That is why exponential change can appear uneventful and then sudden. It did not change rules near the end; the same multiplier was acting on a much larger base.

Real systems rarely grow exponentially forever. Resources, competition, policy or physical limits change the rate. The model is useful over the interval where proportional growth is a reasonable assumption—not as a promise of infinity.

**Takeaway:** when change compounds, ask about the multiplier and doubling time, not only the latest increase.
