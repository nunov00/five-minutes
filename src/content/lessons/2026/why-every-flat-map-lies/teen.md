---
title: Why There Is No Honest Flat Map of Earth
description: Turning a globe into a rectangle always changes size, shape, distance or direction somewhere.
takeaway: Maps are tools with trade-offs, not miniature versions of Earth.
question: What should you ask before using a map to compare two countries?
answer: Which projection it uses and whether that projection preserves the property—such as area—that you want to compare.
searchTerms: [why maps are distorted, mercator explained, map projections]
---
Try flattening the peel of an orange without ripping or stretching it. That is the problem behind every flat world map.

Cartographers use a **projection**: a mathematical rule that transfers points from a curved surface to a plane. The rule can protect some features, but not all of them everywhere.

The Mercator projection protects local angles and directions. Sailors could draw a constant compass course as a straight line. The cost is size: scale increases towards the poles, making places at high latitudes look too large compared with places nearer the equator.

An equal-area projection fixes that comparison, but shapes stretch instead. Other projections balance several distortions without eliminating them.

So the useful question is not “Which map is true?” It is “True enough for what job?” A navigation map and a map comparing land area need different strengths.

**Takeaway:** every flat map edits the globe, and understanding the edit helps you read it correctly.
