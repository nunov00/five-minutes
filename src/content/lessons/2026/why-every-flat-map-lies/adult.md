---
title: Every Flat World Map Must Distort Something
description: Flattening a curved planet forces cartographers to choose which properties to preserve and which to sacrifice.
takeaway: A projection is a purpose-built trade-off, so a map should be judged by the job it was designed to do.
question: Why can no flat world map preserve area, shape, distance and direction everywhere?
answer: A curved surface cannot be flattened without stretching, compressing or tearing relationships somewhere on the map.
searchTerms: [map projection distortion, mercator map, equal area map]
---
Peel an orange and press its skin flat. It must tear, overlap or stretch. A world map faces the same geometric problem: Earth's curved surface cannot become a plane without distortion.

A **projection** is a rule for making that transformation. Different rules protect different properties. An equal-area map preserves relative area but distorts shapes. A conformal projection preserves local angles and shapes but changes scale.

The Mercator projection is conformal. A constant compass bearing appears as a straight line, which made it valuable for navigation. Its scale grows sharply towards the poles, so high-latitude land appears much larger relative to tropical land than it is on a globe.

That does not make Mercator fraudulent; it makes it unsuitable for comparing country sizes. No projection is best for every purpose. A route map, a population map and a classroom world map may need different compromises.
