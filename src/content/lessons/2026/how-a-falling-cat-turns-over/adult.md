A cat that starts falling upside down can turn its feet towards the ground even if it starts with no spin. There is nothing solid to push against. The trick is that the cat can change its shape as it falls.[^1]

## No starting spin

Imagine two people floating face to face. If they grip each other’s hands and twist in opposite directions, each can turn a little. Neither person has pushed against anything outside the pair. Their contributions to **angular momentum**, the quantity that tracks rotation, can cancel even while they are both moving.[^1][^2]

That is the opening a cat has. Over the brief fall, gravity pulls it down but supplies almost no twist about its centre of mass. The front and rear can still move relative to each other. Total angular momentum near zero does not mean every part has to stay still.[^1][^2]

## Make a loop with the bend

Engineers Thomas Kane and M. P. Scher represented the cat with two linked bodies that could bend at the join. To picture the sequence, follow the direction of the rear half’s bend relative to the front half. It points forward first, moves to one side, then back, then to the other side, and finally forward again.[^1]

Here is the part to look for: a sideways bend is an adjustment in a different direction from a forward bend. As the rear moves at the join, the front also moves so their total angular momentum stays at zero. Because the bend keeps changing direction, those adjustments act about different axes. They do not have to undo one another. The model ends with the same bend pointing forward relative to its own body, but with its feet facing the other way relative to the floor.[^1][^2]

Imagine instead that the model bends to one side and retraces its movement exactly. Each adjustment runs in reverse, so that simple out-and-back motion undoes itself. Going around a loop of bends gives a different result. What matters is the sequence of shapes as well as the starting and ending shapes.[^2]

The linked bodies are a model, not a prescription for how every cat moves or a guarantee of a safe landing. They show why there is no contradiction: an object can change which way it faces without gaining total angular momentum, provided its parts move through the right sequence.[^1][^2]
