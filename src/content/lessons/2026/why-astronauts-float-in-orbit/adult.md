An astronaut floats beside a window while Earth passes below. It looks as though gravity has disappeared. Yet gravity remains strong at the height of an orbiting space station. Without its pull, the station would not follow its curved path around Earth.

The missing ingredient is relative motion. On the ground, a dropped object falls towards a floor that stays in place. In orbit, the spacecraft is falling too.

## Falling without arriving

**Free fall** means moving under gravity alone. To a close approximation, the station, its crew and a gently released object share that motion. The object therefore appears to hover beside the astronaut rather than drop to the floor.

Why does the whole scene not crash? The station also travels sideways fast enough that its curved path follows Earth's curvature. It continually falls around the planet rather than reaching the ground.

Real spacecraft are imperfect free-fall environments. Atmospheric drag, vibrations and other small disturbances create residual accelerations. That is why scientists describe their environment as **microgravity**, rather than perfectly undisturbed weightlessness.

Floating inside the cabin reveals how the cabin and its contents move together. It does not show that Earth's gravitational pull has switched off.
