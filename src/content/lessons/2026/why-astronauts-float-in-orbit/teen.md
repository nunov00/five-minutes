Let go of a pencil in your room and it drops. Let go of one inside an orbiting station and it seems to hang beside you. Has travelling into space turned gravity off?

No. Earth's gravity is still strong there. It pulls on the pencil, the astronaut and the station itself.

## The room falls with you

Picture a small cabin freely falling, with air resistance ignored. A person and an object released beside them both accelerate under gravity. The floor falls with them, so the object appears to float inside the cabin.

That shared motion is the key to **free fall**. The space station is doing it continuously. Its sideways speed carries it around Earth's curve while gravity bends its path, so it keeps falling without reaching the ground.

The pencil was moving with the station before anyone let go. Releasing it does not remove that sideways motion. There is no reason for it suddenly to get left behind.

Real stations have small disturbances from air drag and vibrations, so the floating is not perfectly undisturbed. But the central idea holds: the pencil looks almost still because its surroundings are falling with it.
