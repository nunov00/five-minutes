Earth finishes a spin in about 23 hours 56 minutes. Your clock still has almost four minutes to run before completing a day. Where did the difference come from?

The two measurements use different finish lines. A **sidereal day** measures a turn against distant stars. A **solar day** measures the return from one solar noon to the next: the moment the Sun crosses your local north–south line in the sky. Noon here means a Sun alignment, not necessarily 12:00 on a clock.

Imagine a painted arrow on Earth pointing towards the Sun. During one star-relative rotation, the arrow turns through a full circle and points in its original direction. But Earth has also travelled onwards around the Sun. The line joining Earth to the Sun has shifted, so the arrow has not yet caught up with it.

Earth spins and orbits in the same sense when viewed from above the North Pole. It therefore needs a little more rotation to restore noon, rather than a little less.

## The moving finish line

One orbit covers 360 degrees in about 365 days: roughly one degree a day. Earth rotates about 15 degrees an hour. Turning the extra degree therefore takes about one-fifteenth of an hour, or four minutes. The more precise average difference is about 3 minutes 56 seconds.

Those small extra turns add up to a whole rotation over one orbit. A year with roughly 365 solar days therefore contains roughly 366 rotations relative to the stars. No extra sunrise is missing: each Sun-based day includes a small part of that additional rotation.

There is a second complication. The orbit is not perfectly circular, so Earth's orbital speed changes. Its tilted axis also makes orbital motion project unevenly onto the east–west direction used for timekeeping. The daily catch-up is consequently not identical throughout the year.

A **mean solar day** smooths those variations into a 24-hour average. Modern civil time is maintained with atomic clocks, rather than resetting each day to a sundial, but the familiar day retains that averaged Sun-based scale.

The four minutes are the cost of chasing a moving finish line. Earth has completed a spin before it has completed a day.
