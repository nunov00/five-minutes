---
title: Your Shadow Is a Moving Sun Tracker
description: Follow one shadow for a day and it reveals how the Sun's apparent position changes.
takeaway: A moving shadow is evidence of Earth's rotation changing the Sun's apparent direction and height.
question: What does a long shadow tell you about the Sun's position?
answer: The Sun is relatively low in the sky, so its light reaches the object at a shallow angle.
searchTerms: [why shadows move, long shadows, sun tracker]
---
Stand in the same sunny place in the morning and again around midday. Your shadow will have turned, and it will probably be a different length.

The shadow forms on the side away from the Sun. Earth rotates while you stand on its surface, making the Sun appear to cross the sky. As the light direction changes, the shadow points in a new direction.

Angle controls length. A low Sun sends light across the ground at a shallow angle, producing a long shadow. A higher Sun sends light down more steeply, producing a shorter one.

Season and location change the details, so the pattern is not identical everywhere. Still, repeated marks from one fixed object can work like a simple record of the Sun's apparent motion.
