## Candidate briefs — assess these before reading the provisional scores

**Aircraft windows — `legacy-aircraft-pressurisation`**
- Proposed title: **Why aircraft windows have rounded corners**.
- Description/payoff: A familiar detail reveals how openings change stress in a loaded shell, and why repeated loading matters.
- Reason to choose: recognise an object seen during travel and learn a design principle that extends beyond aircraft.
- Visual concept: recognisable cabin window lead; separate, qualitative sharp/rounded cut-out comparison under matched loading; a cycle/fatigue explanation only if it adds a distinct causal step. Up to five useful visuals, no quota.
- Main risk: a predictable “corners are weaker” answer or an accident-led fear hook. Explain the missing reasoning without implying that round windows alone ensure safety. Source-check history carefully.

**Popcorn — `idea-why-popcorn-pops`**
- Proposed title: **How a hard kernel becomes a piece of popcorn**.
- Description/payoff: Follow a familiar kitchen transformation from confined moisture to a ruptured hull and expanded starch.
- Reason to choose: a dramatic change the reader can recognise without specialist interest.
- Visual concept: kernel and popped form together in the lead, followed by an exact cutaway/state sequence. No need to make the reader guess the subject.
- Main risk: drifting into three lessons about expansion, jumping and sound. Keep expansion central; do not claim the research's sound hypothesis as settled fact.

**Steel ships — `idea-why-steel-ships-float`**
- Proposed title: **Why a steel ship floats while a steel bolt sinks**.
- Description/payoff: Understand how a hollow hull can displace enough water to support the ship and its cargo.
- Reason to choose: an immediately understandable apparent contradiction and a principle that can be pictured with a foil boat.
- Visual concept: ship/bolt contrast, then an exact hull/waterline comparison explaining displacement and added cargo.
- Main risk: a school-fact answer readers feel they already know. Do not imply that air supplies the upward force or width alone ensures stability.
